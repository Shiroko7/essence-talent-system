# Domain Expansions and Domain-Bound Abilities

_Design rationale for Essence Dao paths, with Undertow as the worked example._

> 天時不如地利 · "Opportunities of time vouchsafed by Heaven are not equal to advantages of situation afforded by the Earth." Mencius, 2B:1 (Legge)

## In short

Every path has a domain: a Favored Domain (Primordial), Consecrated Ground (Divine) or Sanctum (Immortal). At least 40% of a path's techniques below Grandmaster (actives and passives) should behave differently there. Spells and cantrips never do.

- The **weaker** ones get a **Domain Expansion**. They work everywhere, and on the domain they add one thing.
- Techniques **too strong for their tier** get a **restore**: nerfed everywhere, full strength on the domain.
- One or two of the **strongest techniques**, Grandmaster or higher, are **domain-bound**. They work only on the domain.
- **Spells and cantrips** get no domain line at all, neither a Domain Expansion nor domain-bound.
- **Everything else** works the same wherever you stand.

A Domain Expansion is one line. The player decides it when they use the ability, and it never adds a roll.

## The three kinds

| | Domain Expansion | Restore | Domain-bound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Away from the domain | Works as written | Works, nerfed | Can't be used |
| On the domain | Adds one thing | Full strength, as before the nerf | Works |
| Tiers | Any, mostly Initiate to Master | Any below Grandmaster | Grandmaster or higher |
| Which abilities | The weakest third of each tier's techniques | Techniques too strong for their tier | The strongest one or two techniques |
| Share of a path | At least 40% of its techniques below Grandmaster | Counts toward the 40% | 1 or 2 entries |
| How it's written | A last line: **Consecrated Ground.** Its damage dice are d10s. | A last line that gives back what the nerf took: **Consecrated Ground.** It doesn't require concentration. | A tag in the header, then: You can use this only on your Consecrated Ground. |

The label is the family's own word: **Favored Domain.**, **Consecrated Ground.** or **Sanctum.**

### Restores

A restore is for a technique that's too strong for its tier. It's nerfed everywhere, and its domain line gives back exactly what the nerf took, so on the domain it works as it did before. Two limits keep it from widening the gap between good and bad picks:

- **Never above the old version.** The line only gives back what the nerf removed. Its weight can exceed the tier's usual cap, because it adds no power the path didn't already have.
- **Worth it away from home.** The nerfed version must still earn its slot.

Ruin's Mark for Death (concentration, none on Consecrated Ground) and Blood Tithe (temporary hit points equal to your PB, your character level at home) are the first restores. Lunar's Waning Moon Sabers (an action, a bonus action on Consecrated Ground) gives back an action cost. Rule 6's ban on faster casting applies to Domain Expansions, not to a restore returning what its nerf took.

## Why it's built this way

### Weak abilities get help, strong abilities get a leash

Both kinds work the same lever from opposite ends. Domain Expansions lift the bottom of the power curve, so a narrow or underpowered pick still earns its place when the fight comes to you. Domain-bound puts a condition on the top of the curve, and that condition is what lets an ability stronger than its tier exist at all.

Doing it the other way round breaks both ends. A Domain Expansion on an already strong ability widens the gap between good and bad picks. A weak ability that is also domain-bound is a trap that nobody should take.

### Some, not all

If every ability changes at home, home becomes the baseline and everywhere else feels like a penalty. The DM would have to ask "whose domain is this?" before every encounter, and players would feel weak on the road. At around 40%, the domain is a high point the party plans for, not something they need just to function.

### Why domain-bound waits for Grandmaster

Before Grandmaster, whether you're on your domain depends entirely on the story, so a domain-only ability could go unused for whole sessions. At Grandmaster, every Attunement gives the cultivator a way to bring the domain to the fight:

| Attunement | Grandmaster benefit | What it means for domain-bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elemental (Primordial) | Domain Manifestation: once per day, the area within 1 mile becomes your Favored Domain for 1 hour | At least one fight a day |
| Portfolio (Divine) | Consecration: the same, for Consecrated Ground | At least one fight a day |
| Dao (Immortal) | Dao Domain: once per day, you are in your Sanctum for 1 hour, wherever you are, and it never collapses in a clash | At least one fight a day, which a clash can't take away |

So a domain-bound ability is guaranteed one big fight a day, which is about how often a cultivator would use their strongest ability anyway. That limit is what pays for its power.

### Only a few

Domain-bound abilities should feel like the god or the element answering, not like the normal kit. If most of a Grandmaster's abilities were domain-bound, their whole kit would hang on one Consecration a day and they'd be weak in every other fight. One or two techniques per path keeps them as the climax.

### Never spells or cantrips

Spells and cantrips get no domain line at all, neither a Domain Expansion nor domain-bound. They come from 5e already priced to work anywhere, with their own scaling in upcasting and cantrip dice. A Domain Expansion on one is a free upcast on top of that, and a spell that only worked at home would be a dead pick on most days. Domain lines are for techniques, which are written around the path itself.

## Rules

1. **One domain.** Domain Expansions and domain-bound abilities use your Attunement's domain, the single domain the Attunement rules already give you. A cultivator who knows Undertow but is attuned to Lunar gets Lunar's Domain Expansions under the moon and no Undertow Domain Expansions anywhere.
2. **Checked on use.** An ability gets its Domain Expansion, or can be used at all if it's domain-bound, if you're in your domain when you use it. The DM says at initiative which parts of the battlefield count. A passive applies while you're there.
3. **Fixed on use.** A Domain Expansion lasts as long as the effect it came with, even if you leave the domain. A domain-bound effect that's already running keeps running.
4. **Only your Attunement makes a domain.** Your own abilities never turn a place into your domain: your ink, conjured water or a grapple doesn't make the Deep. Consecration, Domain Manifestation and Dao Domain do.
5. **One line per technique.** A technique has at most one domain line, and spells and cantrips have none.
6. **Never.** No Legendary Resistance, no legendary actions, no extra action, bonus action or reaction, no faster casting time, no free Dash, Disengage or Hide, and no extra attack. A Domain Expansion may lift a requirement the ability itself sets (Siphon Jet works out of water), because that only switches on what the ability already does.

## Table speed

Domain lines exist to make a fight feel different, not slower. Every line has to pass four checks.

1. **One effect.** If it needs "and" twice, it's two Domain Expansions. Pick one.
2. **Decided on use.** The player says it when they declare the ability. Nothing carries over to remember next round.
3. **No new rolls.** A Domain Expansion may change a roll that's already being made (more dice of the same kind, a bigger die, a higher DC, advantage on the d20) or remove one. It never adds one.
4. **Readable at a glance.** It's written like a "Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot" line, which everyone at the table already knows how to read.

**Banned outright:** rolling damage twice and keeping the higher; rerolling dice; minimum die results ("treat a 1 as a 2"); exploding dice; an extra saving throw or contest; stacks, tokens or counters; a Domain Expansion that triggers off another Domain Expansion; any Domain Expansion that asks "is the target also…?"

**Nothing to track.** A Domain Expansion can't leave a bonus that has to be remembered until your next turn. A defensive Domain Expansion applies only to the attack that triggered it.

**Best of all** are Domain Expansions that remove a roll without taking away the target's chance, which makes the table faster on the domain, not slower.

| Lift a requirement | It works out of water too. / It needs no water source. | ½–1 | Abilities that need a particular environment | The most elegant Domain Expansion, because the domain supplies what the ability needs. |

### Considered and cut

| Modifier | Why it's out |
| --- | --- |
| No repeat saves | It takes away the target's way out of a condition. |
| Sure hit ("it can't miss", "no saving throw") | It takes away the roll. Half on a miss replaces it: the roll stays, and a miss isn't wasted. |
| Cheaper ("costs 1 less Essence") | Essence costs stay fixed. The only cost Domain Expansion is Free, and only at Initiate and Adept. |
| Lasting guard ("+2 AC until the start of your next turn") | It has to be tracked. Guard survives only as a reaction against the attack that triggered it. |
| Any line on a spell or cantrip | 5e already prices spells to work anywhere, with their own scaling. Undertow's nine spell Domain Expansions were removed for this. |
| No components, steady focus | They only ever fit spells. |

Hadal Domain's pressure, which no roll avoids, is a domain-bound technique designed that way on purpose. It isn't a Domain Expansion and doesn't set a precedent for one.

## Pricing

### A Domain Expansion is worth about one spell level

The 2024 Player's Handbook already prices "a bit more" in its upcast lines, so Domain Expansions use the same scale.

| Spell (2024 PHB) | Base | One level higher |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fireball | 8d6 | +1d6 (+12%) |
| Shatter | 3d8 | +1d8 (+33%) |
| Inflict Wounds | 2d10 | +1d10 (+50%) |
| Magic Missile | 3 darts | +1 dart (+33%) |
| Hold Person | 1 target | +1 target (+100%) |

A light Domain Expansion is worth less than a level, a standard Domain Expansion about one, and a heavy Domain Expansion two.

### Two prices to hold

- **An ability must be worth its slot without its Domain Expansion.** The Domain Expansion is a bonus, never the reason to take the ability.
- **A domain-bound technique may sit about one spell level above its tier,** because it's only guaranteed one fight a day.

### Weight by tier

- **Initiate techniques that run below the curve:** up to heavy.
- **Adept and Master:** light or standard.
- **Grandmaster and above:** light only. The strongest technique can go domain-bound instead.

### Worked numbers

- **Grasping Tentacle** (Initiate, die step): 3d8 (13.5) becomes 3d12 (19.5), +44%, and it still grapples.
- **Hadal Domain** (Grandmaster, domain-bound, a bonus action and 4 Essence for 1 minute): 3d8 (13.5) to any number of creatures when it wakes and each round after, with no roll. Each round that's about 60% of a 6th or 7th-level area spell, with no further action and no concentration. Against four creatures over four rounds it deals about 216; a 7th-level Fire Storm for the same 4 Essence deals about 120, once.

## Choosing which abilities get lines

1. List the path's techniques by tier and rank each tier by power. Leave spells and cantrips out.
2. The bottom third of each tier are Domain Expansion candidates. Find each one's weakness and let the Domain Expansion fix exactly that: a narrow requirement gets lifted, low damage gets dice, one target gets more, an easy save gets disadvantage.
3. The strongest one or two techniques at Grandmaster or above can become domain-bound. Check that the limit makes sense in the fiction: Hadal Domain is the deep itself rising.
4. Leave the rest plain once at least 40% of the techniques below Grandmaster have a line.
5. Write it in the path's voice. A Domain Expansion should feel like the place answering, not a generic +1.

## Family notes

**Primordial.** Essence Supremacy (Great Grandmaster) already ignores resistance at home, so skip damage-type and resistance Domain Expansions. Domain Movement (Adept) already grants movement speeds at home, so Water and Sky shouldn't spend Domain Expansions on swim or fly speeds.

**Divine.** Several Consecrated Grounds include "wherever it is happening": Ruin's murder scene, Providence's suffering. Rule 4 is what stops a cultivator from creating their own domain mid-fight. Undertow avoids the problem by wording its last example as "any wreck or shore where someone drowned" instead of "anywhere someone is being dragged under".

**Immortal.** From Grandmaster, Dao Domain gives an hour a day like Domain Manifestation and Consecration, so Immortal paths take Domain Expansions and domain-bound abilities the same way as the other families. What sets them apart is that a clash can't take the hour away: Dao Domain never collapses, even when its side loses, though it still joins clashes and can collapse other domains. Their Dao Heart belongs to them alone.

## In the data

Two optional fields in an entry's YAML block under `data/cultivation/v2/`:

```yaml
domain: expansion      # or: restore, bound
domainText: Its damage dice are d10s.
```

- The app prints the line under the ability's text, labelled with the family's domain word (Favored Domain, Consecrated Ground, or Sanctum). A domain-bound technique gets "Domain-bound. You can use it only on your …" instead.
- `bun run generate:cultivation` rejects a domain line on a spell or cantrip, a Domain Expansion or restore without `domainText`, `domainText` without a kind, and anything domain-bound below Grandmaster.
- The Rules page lists the domain-line rules under every Attunement.

## Undertow, worked

9 techniques below Grandmaster, 5 with a Domain Expansion (56%, target 40%), plus Hadal Domain, domain-bound at Grandmaster. Its 22 spells and cantrips have no domain lines.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | :-: |
| Initiate | Siphon Jet | Domain Expansion | It works out of water too. | 1 |
| Initiate | Pressure Sense | Domain Expansion | Both ranges double. | ½ |
| Initiate | Grasping Tentacle | Domain Expansion | Its damage dice are d12s. | 1 |
| Adept | Ink Jet | Domain Expansion | You move up to your full speed instead. | ½ |
| Adept | Angler's Lure | Domain Expansion | Creatures that can't be charmed are affected too. | ½ |
| Grandmaster | Hadal Domain | Bound | A bonus action and 4 Essence wake the deep for 1 minute. | – |

**Plain (4):** Ink Sac (Initiate), Drag Below (Adept), Kraken's Grasp and Drowning Grip (Master). Each already blinds, restrains or silences on a single roll, which is strong for its tier.

Hadal Domain is active rather than passive: a bonus action and its Essence wake the deep for 1 minute, so it can't run every fight for free. The first pulse hits as it wakes, so the bonus action costs tempo, not damage. Below the Light only changes ink; treating dry land as underwater would have pulled creatures down into solid rock.

Maelstrom stays in Tempest. It's a spell, so it gets no domain line.

## Settled

1. **Rule 1:** Domain Expansions use your Attunement's domain only.
2. **Spells and cantrips:** no domain line of any kind. Undertow's Great Grandmaster technique won't be domain-bound either.
3. **The Deep:** its last example is "any wreck or shore where someone drowned".
4. **Elsewhere:** Maelstrom stays in Tempest, and Water's former Echoes of the Deep talent becomes Tidespeech.
