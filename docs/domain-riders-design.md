# Domain Riders and Domain-Bound Abilities

_Design rationale for Essence Dao paths, with Undertow as the worked example._

> 天時不如地利 · "Opportunities of time vouchsafed by Heaven are not equal to advantages of situation afforded by the Earth." Mencius, 2B:1 (Legge)

## In short

Every path has a domain: a Favored Domain (Primordial), Consecrated Ground (Divine) or Sanctum (Immortal). About half of a path's abilities should behave differently there.

- The **weaker** ones get a **domain rider**. They work everywhere, and on the domain they add one thing.
- One or two of the **strongest techniques**, Grandmaster or higher, are **domain-bound**. They work only on the domain. Spells and cantrips never are.
- **Everything else** works the same wherever you stand.

A rider is one line. The player decides it when they use the ability, and it never adds a roll.

## The two kinds

| | Domain rider | Domain-bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Away from the domain | Works as written | Can't be used |
| On the domain | Adds one thing | Works |
| Tiers | Any, mostly Initiate to Master | Grandmaster or higher |
| Which abilities | The weakest third of each tier | The strongest one or two techniques; never a spell or cantrip |
| Share of a path | About 45% | 1 or 2 entries |
| How it's written | A last line: **Consecrated Ground.** Its damage dice are d10s. | A tag in the header, then: You can use this only on your Consecrated Ground. |

The label is the family's own word: **Favored Domain.**, **Consecrated Ground.** or **Sanctum.**

## Why it's built this way

### Weak abilities get help, strong abilities get a leash

Both kinds work the same lever from opposite ends. Riders lift the bottom of the power curve, so a narrow or underpowered pick still earns its place when the fight comes to you. Domain-bound puts a condition on the top of the curve, and that condition is what lets an ability stronger than its tier exist at all.

Doing it the other way round breaks both ends. A rider on an already strong ability widens the gap between good and bad picks. A weak ability that is also domain-bound is a trap that nobody should take.

### Half, not all

If every ability changes at home, home becomes the baseline and everywhere else feels like a penalty. The DM would have to ask "whose domain is this?" before every encounter, and players would feel weak on the road. At about half, the domain is a high point the party plans for, not something they need just to function.

### Why domain-bound waits for Grandmaster

Before Grandmaster, whether you're on your domain depends entirely on the story, so a domain-only ability could go unused for whole sessions. At Grandmaster, every Attunement gives the cultivator a way to bring the domain to the fight:

| Attunement | Grandmaster benefit | What it means for domain-bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elemental (Primordial) | Domain Manifestation: once per day, the area within 1 mile becomes your Favored Domain for 1 hour | At least one fight a day |
| Portfolio (Divine) | Consecration: the same, for Consecrated Ground | At least one fight a day |
| Dao (Immortal) | Dao Domain: you are always in your Sanctum | Every fight (see Family notes) |

So a domain-bound ability is guaranteed one big fight a day, which is about how often a cultivator would use their strongest ability anyway. That limit is what pays for its power.

### Only a few

Domain-bound abilities should feel like the god or the element answering, not like the normal kit. If most of a Grandmaster's abilities were domain-bound, their whole kit would hang on one Consecration a day and they'd be weak in every other fight. One or two techniques per path keeps them as the climax.

### Never spells

Spells and cantrips are never domain-bound. They come from 5e already priced to work anywhere, and a spell that only worked at home would be a dead pick on most days. Domain-bound is for techniques written around the domain itself. A strong spell at Grandmaster or above simply stays plain.

## Rules

1. **One domain.** Riders and domain-bound abilities use your Attunement's domain, the single domain the Attunement rules already give you. A cultivator who knows Undertow but is attuned to Lunar gets Lunar's riders under the moon and no Undertow riders anywhere.
2. **Checked on use.** An ability gets its rider, or can be used at all if it's domain-bound, if you're in your domain when you use it. The DM says at initiative which parts of the battlefield count. A passive applies while you're there.
3. **Fixed on use.** A rider lasts as long as the effect it came with, even if you leave the domain. A domain-bound effect that's already running keeps running.
4. **Only your Attunement makes a domain.** Your own abilities never turn a place into your domain: your ink, conjured water or a grapple doesn't make the Deep. Consecration, Domain Manifestation and Dao Domain do.
5. **One line per ability.** An ability has at most one domain line. A rider stacks with upcasting.
6. **Never.** No Legendary Resistance, no legendary actions, no extra action, bonus action or reaction, no faster casting time, no free Dash, Disengage or Hide, and no extra attack. A rider may lift a requirement the ability itself sets (Siphon Jet works out of water), because that only switches on what the ability already does.

## Table speed

Domain lines exist to make a fight feel different, not slower. Every line has to pass four checks.

1. **One effect.** If it needs "and" twice, it's two riders. Pick one.
2. **Decided on use.** The player says it when they declare the ability. Nothing carries over to remember next round.
3. **No new rolls.** A rider may change a roll that's already being made (more dice of the same kind, a bigger die, a higher DC, advantage on the d20) or remove one (no concentration, no save). It never adds one.
4. **Readable at a glance.** It's written like a "Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot" line, which everyone at the table already knows how to read.

**Banned outright:** rolling damage twice and keeping the higher; rerolling dice; minimum die results ("treat a 1 as a 2"); exploding dice; an extra saving throw or contest; stacks, tokens or counters; a rider that triggers off another rider; any rider that asks "is the target also…?"

**Nothing to track.** A rider can't leave a bonus that has to be remembered until your next turn. A defensive rider applies only to the attack that triggered it.

**Best of all** are riders that remove a roll without taking away the target's chance. "No concentration" means no concentration saves, which makes the table faster on the domain, not slower.

### Considered and cut

| Modifier | Why it's out |
| --- | --- |
| No repeat saves | It takes away the target's way out of a condition. |
| Sure hit ("it can't miss", "no saving throw") | It takes away the roll. Half on a miss replaces it: the roll stays, and a miss isn't wasted. |
| Cheaper ("costs 1 less Essence") | Essence costs stay fixed. The only cost rider is Free, and only at Initiate and Adept. |
| Lasting guard ("+2 AC until the start of your next turn") | It has to be tracked. Guard survives only as a reaction against the attack that triggered it. |

Hadal Domain's pressure, which no roll avoids, is a domain-bound technique designed that way on purpose. It isn't a rider and doesn't set a precedent for one.

## Pricing

### A rider is worth about one spell level

The 2024 Player's Handbook already prices "a bit more" in its upcast lines, so riders use the same scale.

| Spell (2024 PHB) | Base | One level higher |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fireball | 8d6 | +1d6 (+12%) |
| Shatter | 3d8 | +1d8 (+33%) |
| Inflict Wounds | 2d10 | +1d10 (+50%) |
| Magic Missile | 3 darts | +1 dart (+33%) |
| Hold Person | 1 target | +1 target (+100%) |

A light rider is worth less than a level, a standard rider about one, and a heavy rider two.

### Two prices to hold

- **An ability must be worth its slot without its rider.** The rider is a bonus, never the reason to take the ability.
- **A domain-bound technique may sit about one spell level above its tier,** because it's only guaranteed one fight a day.

### Weight by tier

- **Initiate abilities and spells that run below the curve:** up to heavy.
- **Adept and Master:** light or standard.
- **Grandmaster and above:** light only. The strongest technique can go domain-bound instead; strong spells stay plain.

### Worked numbers

- **Water Whip** (cantrip): 3d6 (10.5) at 11th level becomes 3d10 (16.5), level with Fire Bolt, and it still pulls.
- **Tentacle Lash** (4th, half on a miss): 5d8 (22.5) at a 65% hit chance averages 14.6 a turn. Half damage on a miss raises that to 18.6, +27%.
- **Hadal Domain** (Grandmaster, domain-bound, a bonus action and 4 Essence for 1 minute): 3d8 (13.5) to any number of creatures when it wakes and each round after, with no roll. Each round that's about 60% of a 6th or 7th-level area spell, with no further action and no concentration. Against four creatures over four rounds it deals about 216; a 7th-level Fire Storm for the same 4 Essence deals about 120, once.

## Choosing which abilities get lines

1. List the path by tier and rank each tier by power.
2. The bottom third of each tier are rider candidates. Find each one's weakness and let the rider fix exactly that: a narrow requirement gets lifted, low damage gets dice, one target gets more, an easy save gets disadvantage.
3. The strongest one or two techniques at Grandmaster or above can become domain-bound. Spells and cantrips never do. Check that the limit makes sense in the fiction: Hadal Domain is the deep itself rising.
4. Leave the rest plain, and stop at about half the path.
5. Write it in the path's voice. A rider should feel like the place answering, not a generic +1.

## Family notes

**Primordial.** Essence Supremacy (Great Grandmaster) already ignores resistance at home, so skip damage-type and resistance riders. Domain Movement (Adept) already grants movement speeds at home, so Water and Sky shouldn't spend riders on swim or fly speeds.

**Divine.** Several Consecrated Grounds include "wherever it is happening": Ruin's murder scene, Providence's suffering. Rule 4 is what stops a cultivator from creating their own domain mid-fight. Undertow avoids the problem by wording its last example as "any wreck or shore where someone drowned" instead of "anywhere someone is being dragged under".

**Immortal.** From Grandmaster, Dao Domain means an attuned cultivator is always in their Sanctum. Immortal riders therefore become permanent at 13th level, and a domain-bound Immortal ability would have no limit at all. Immortal paths should use light riders only and no domain-bound abilities. Their identity is that the place moves into you.

## In the data

Two optional fields in an entry's YAML block under `data/cultivation/v2/`:

```yaml
domain: rider          # or: bound
domainText: Its damage dice are d10s.
```

- The app prints the line under the ability's text, labelled with the family's domain word (Favored Domain, Consecrated Ground, or Sanctum). A domain-bound technique gets "Domain-bound. You can use it only on your …" instead.
- `bun run generate:cultivation` rejects a rider without `domainText`, `domainText` without a kind, and anything domain-bound that is a spell, a cantrip, or below Grandmaster.
- The Rules page lists the domain-line rules under every Attunement.

## Settled

1. **Rule 1:** riders use your Attunement's domain only.
2. **Domain-bound:** techniques only, never spells or cantrips. Undertow's Great Grandmaster technique won't be domain-bound either.
3. **The Deep:** its last example is "any wreck or shore where someone drowned".
4. **Elsewhere:** Maelstrom stays in Tempest, and Water's former Echoes of the Deep talent becomes Tidespeech.
